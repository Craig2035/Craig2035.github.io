###### Data in Education

**Executive summary:**

A showcase of how data can transform educational decision‑making, pastoral insight, and operational planning.  
This project demonstrates how GCSE results, student demographics, attendance, and progress data can be combined into a clear, actionable reporting model for school leaders.

---

### High School GCSE Tracking and Results Dashboard

**Summary of Project**

Annual tracking and reviewing of GCSE results in UK schools has often relied on spreadsheets or exam‑board‑specific tools.  
Using Power BI, I created a multi‑page dashboard model that provides:

- Whole‑school executive overview  
- Subject‑level performance with filtering by gender, SEND, EAL, and Pupil Premium  
- Subject comparison and ranking  
- Individual student performance for tailored reporting  

**Note:**  All student names and data were AI‑generated to represent a normally distributed cohort from a non‑selective school.

### Overview

![Overview Dashboard](/assets/images/GCSE_overview.png)

Impact scores of results are presented in a series of KPI cards at the top of the page. The main column chart presents distribution of whole school results, while progress through mock series is tracked in the line chart bottom left. The donut charts mid right give year group demographics scatter chart bottom right, while the scatter chart bottom right shows the impact on average GCSE score with attendance, which decreases below the government target of 95% attendance. 

### Subject Performance 

![Subject Performance Dashboard](/assets/images/GCSE_subject_performance.png)

Tracking each year is essential for planning and future progress by heads of department and whole school academic leads. Subject performance can be analysed at a top level or by demographic grouping. The subject performance KPI card gives a quick overview of results with the column chart and line chart showing breakdown by grade and tracking of progress respectively. 

The optional step 2 filters buttons allow identification of high or low performing demographic subgroups within the subject.
